Isaiah's list of woes starts out feeling like judgment against Israel and Judah, until their attitudes and actions begin to feel a little more personal: not paying attention to the work of God's hands, dragging sin behind us, demanding that God show Himself on our timetable, calling evil good, and being wise in our own eyes. And right there in the middle is the God we've been learning to see in His fullness—the gentle, loving Shepherd who is also the Lord of Armies, holy and righteous in His judgment.
